The Life

Chapters of a Life in Flight

Six turns in one trajectory — a life that moved from a small island town to the frontier of Indian science, and back to a classroom.

Rameswaram

Roots on the Coast

Born to a boat-owner and a homemaker on the temple island of Rameswaram, young Kalam sold newspapers before school to help support his family. The rhythm of the sea and the discipline of early mornings shaped a boy who found wonder in flight the first time he watched birds glide over the Bay of Bengal.

MIT, Chennai

The Making of an Engineer

Physics at St. Joseph's College gave way to a deeper pull toward the sky. At the Madras Institute of Technology, Kalam chose aerospace engineering — a decision he later called the moment his life found its true bearing, even after a near-miss with becoming a fighter pilot.

ISRO

Building India's Reach to Orbit

At the fledgling Indian National Committee for Space Research, and later ISRO, Kalam worked on the country's first indigenous satellite launch vehicles. He led the team behind SLV-III, the rocket that placed Rohini in orbit and proved India could build its own way into space.

DRDO

The Missile Man

Leading the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, Kalam directed the design of Agni and Prithvi — projects that earned him a name that would follow him for life. The title was never about weapons to him; it was about proving a nation's engineers could match the world's best.

2002 – 2007

The People's President

Elected the 11th President of India with rare cross-party support, Kalam turned Rashtrapati Bhavan into a space for young minds — hosting students, scientists, and children, and answering thousands of letters by hand. He remained, by his own description, a servant of the republic first.

2007 – 2015

Teacher, to the Last Lecture

After his term ended, Kalam returned to what he loved most — teaching. He lectured at IITs, IIMs, and universities worldwide, wrote extensively for young readers, and passed away mid-sentence while addressing students at IIM Shillong on 27 July 2015.

The Trajectory

Launch to Legacy

Nine waypoints along one flight path — drawn as you scroll, the way a mission's course is drawn on a control-room screen.

  1. 1931

    Born in Rameswaram

    Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam is born into a modest Tamil Muslim family on the temple island of Rameswaram.

  2. 1955

    Aerospace Engineering, MIT Chennai

    Graduates from the Madras Institute of Technology, choosing aircraft and rockets over a career in the Air Force.

  3. 1963

    Joins India's Space Programme

    Begins work with the Indian National Committee for Space Research, the seed that would grow into ISRO.

  4. 1980

    SLV-III Reaches Orbit

    Leads the team that places the Rohini satellite into orbit using India's first indigenous launch vehicle.

  5. 1983

    Integrated Guided Missile Programme

    Takes charge of developing Agni and Prithvi, earning the name that would define his public identity.

  6. 1998

    Pokhran-II

    Serves as Chief Scientific Adviser during the Pokhran-II tests, cementing his role at the centre of India's defence science.

  7. 2002

    11th President of India

    Sworn in with support that crossed party lines, becoming known within months as the People's President.

  8. 2007

    Return to Teaching

    Leaves Rashtrapati Bhavan and returns full-time to lecturing, writing, and mentoring students across the country.

  9. 2015

    The Last Lecture

    Passes away on 27 July while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong — teaching, as he had asked to, until the very end.

Recognition

Honours & Instruments of a Legacy

Medals mark moments. What they measure here is a habit of showing up — in labs, classrooms, and letters to strangers.

1997

Bharat Ratna

India's highest civilian award, in recognition of a lifetime spent building the country's scientific capability.

2 + 1

Padma Vibhushan & Padma Bhushan

Two of India's earliest civilian honours, awarded well before his presidency, for contributions to science and engineering.

15+

Books Authored

From Wings of Fire to Ignited Minds, Kalam wrote for the young reader as much as the scientist.

40+

Honorary Doctorates

Conferred by universities across India and abroad, in science, law, and the humanities alike.
